## 2. Other Maharashtra labyrinths
- Kalambi I-IV, "Kodyache Maal" pasture, ~1.6 km NE of Kalambi, Khatav tehsil, Satara district; ~2000 years old (believed);
  possibly markers for merchants going inland to Kolhapur and Karad in the Satavahana period; similar labyrinths in Sangli,
  Kolhapur and Solapur districts. Local names: "Kode" (puzzle), "chakravyuh", "manchakra", "yamadwar".
  Source: Wikipedia "Khatav" (refs: Caerdroia 52, Journal of Mazes & Labyrinths; TOI 19 Dec 2025).
- Wikipedia "Labyrinth": securely datable labyrinths in India appear from ~250 BC; Indian labyrinths often follow the
  Classical (Cretan) pattern; in manuscripts they are often called Chakravyuha (Mahabharata battle formation).

## 3. Crete / Knossos
- Silver coins of Knossos show the 7-course "Classical" labyrinth; on coins from ~430 BC (Wikipedia "Labyrinth", Kern 2000).
  The photographed coin is c. 400 BC, Heraklion Archaeological Museum. VO: "2400 saal purane Greek sikke" (c. 400 BCE).
- Myth: Daedalus built the Labyrinth for King Minos to hold the Minotaur (Wikipedia "Knossos").

## 4. Trade in the Satavahana era
- Strabo (Geography II.5.12): when Gallus was prefect of Egypt (c. 26-24 BCE), up to 120 ships a year sailed from
  Myos Hormos (Egypt, Red Sea) to India. Wikipedia "Indo-Roman trade relations". VO: "Strabo ke mutabik har saal 120 jahaz
  Egypt se India".
- Pliny: India, China and Arabia take 100 million sesterces a year from Rome (not used in VO).
- Satavahana lead coin of Vasisthiputra Sri Pulamavi with an Indian ship, 1st-2nd c. CE (British Museum). Wikipedia
  "Satavahana dynasty". Pulamavi c. 96-119 CE (Sen) / from c. 110 CE (Higham).
- Paithan (Pratishthana) = Satavahana capital on the Godavari (Wikipedia "Paithan"). Ter = Tagara of the Periplus,
  peak prosperity in the Satavahana period (Wikipedia "Ter, Maharashtra", Casson 2012). VO: "Paithan aur Ter jaise bazaar".

## 5. Pompeii Lakshmi
- Ivory statuette, 24.5 cm, found Oct 1938 by Amedeo Maiuri in Pompeii (destroyed 79 CE); Naples National Archaeological
  Museum (Secret Museum), inv. 149425; dated 1st half of 1st c. CE. Identified as Lakshmi (Maiuri 1939) - also called a
  yakshi ("Pompeii Yakshi") -> VO "Lakshmi ya Yakshi". Production place now thought to be Bhokardan, Maharashtra
  (Dhavalikar), since an almost identical figurine was found there; Satavahana territory. VO says "shayad ... Bhokardan".
  Source: Wikipedia "Pompeii Lakshmi".
