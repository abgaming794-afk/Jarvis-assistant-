|---|---|---|
| Indian Ocean is named after India, which projects into it | Wikipedia "Indian Ocean" > Etymology (cites Online Etymology Dictionary, Harper) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean | "teer ki tarah ghusa hua" = our wording for "projects into it" |
| Present name known since at least 1515, Latin "Oceanus Orientalis Indicus" ("Indian Eastern Ocean") | same (Etymonline: "first attested 1515 in Modern Latin") | we do not name the 1515 document (Etymonline does not) |
| Earlier "Eastern Ocean", as opposed to "Western Ocean" (Atlantic) | same | European usage, still in mid-18th c. |
| Chinese explorers (Zheng He, Ming, 15th c.) called it "Western Oceans" | same (Hui 2010); Zheng He voyages 1405-1433: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_He | route on map = approximate (Suzhou - Malacca - Calicut - Hormuz) |
| Greeks called the part they knew the Erythraean Sea | same; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythraean_Sea ("Erythrà Thálassa", lit. Red Sea; the Periplus used it for Red Sea + Arabian Sea + Persian Gulf + Indian Ocean) | |
| Periplus of the Erythraean Sea, 1st century CE, Greek-speaking merchants' guide (Alexandria) | https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_the_Erythraean_Sea | "lagbhag 2000 saal pehle" = 1st c. CE |
