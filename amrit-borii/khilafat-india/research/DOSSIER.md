# DOSSIER — "Arab Khilafat India kyun nahi jeet paayi?"

AMRIT BORI long-form documentary · 12-15 min · research date 2026-11-15

**Core question (viewer search):** "why the arab caliphate never conquered india". vidIQ: volume 55, competition 29/100,
~5.2K searches/month. A related search, "why everyone invaded india", has competition 20.

**One-line answer (verdict):** For about 100 years (c. 636-776) the Arab caliphate tried to take India by sea, through
Sindh and through Kabul. It held only Sindh and Multan. Four things stopped it: (1) the Kabul-Zabul wall in the
north-west, (2) the harsh desert route through Makran and Sindh, (3) strong Indian kingdoms that beat the Arabs again
and again between 725 and 776 (Pratihara, Chalukya, Kashmir, Saindhava and others), and (4) the caliphate's own
overstretch: it was losing on several fronts at once in 724-741 and fell in a revolution in 750.
**Important nuance:** much later, *Turkic* rulers conquered north India: Mahmud of Ghazni from 1001, Muhammad Ghuri at
Tarain in 1192. They were not the Arab caliphate. The video must say this clearly so it does not mislead.

## 3. The "why" — reasons with evidence

1. **The Kabul-Zabul wall.** The normal invasion road into India (Kabul, then the Khyber) stayed shut for more than
   200 years. The Turk Shahis and Zunbils beat the "Army of Destruction" (698) and others. So the Arabs had to come
   the long way, through Makran and Sindh. [S4][S5]
2. **Geography.** Caliph Umar had already halted expeditions there, "calling it an inhospitable and poor land" [S6].
   The route was the Makran coast, then Sindh, then the Thar desert, before reaching rich Gujarat and Malwa.
   *(No area numbers for the Thar unless sourced.)*
3. **Indian kingdoms fought back many times, on many fronts.** Nagabhata I (c. 725), Jayabhata IV (736), Navsari (739),
   Chittor (740), al-Hakam killed (740), and the Saindhava fleet (756, 776). All gains in Rajasthan and Gujarat were
   lost by 743. Pratihara cavalry was famous even among Arab writers in 851. [S2][S8][S15]
4. **The caliphate was overstretched.** Under Caliph Hisham (724-743) it was defeated on many fronts at once: the Day of
   Thirst (724), the Khazars (730), the Defile (731), Tours (732), Akroinon (739) and the Berber Revolt (740-741). The 3,000
   reinforcements meant for Sindh in 737 were sent to Iraq instead. Blankinship: "the military disasters of Hisham's
   reign brought about the Umayyad dynasty's demise." [S9][S2]
5. **Mutiny and fatigue.** Arab records say troops from Syria and Yemen abandoned their posts in India and refused to
   return. Blankinship thinks the cause was internal to the Arab forces. [S2]
6. **Then the empire itself fell (750)** and later broke up. Sindh split into small emirates around 854. [S3][S10]
7. **Not just enemies: trade.** The Rashtrakutas welcomed Arab merchants, allowed mosques in Konkan ports, and around
   944 Saymur had about 10,000 Muslims (al-Masudi). Arab writers praised the Balhara king. This is a balance point:
   the relationship was not only war. [S15]

## 4. Disputed or unverified claims (do NOT state as fact)

| Claim | Status | How to say it |
|---|---|---|
| "Battle of Rajasthan, 738" was one big battle | It is a modern label; the sources describe a **series of battles in 725-743**. Wikipedia redirects "Battle of Rajasthan" to "Umayyad campaigns in India". | "Jise aaj log 'Battle of Rajasthan' kehte hain, wo asal mein ek nahi, kai ladaiyon ka silsila tha." |
| A grand alliance of Nagabhata, Bappa Rawal and Pulakeshin beat 30,000+ Arabs | Troop numbers come from popular sites; **no scholarly source** found. | Do not give numbers. |
| Bappa Rawal killed Junayd | **False/unsupported.** Junayd was replaced in 726, and Tamim followed. | Do not say it. |
| Bappa Rawal founded Rawalpindi | **Unsupported.** | Do not say it. |
| Hajjaj spent 60 million dirhams and got 120 million back | It is in al-Baladhuri, but translations differ on whether 60 million was the cost or the gain. | Optional: "Baladhuri ke mutabik Hajjaj ne daawa kiya ki kharche se doguna wapas mila" + label "(daawa)". **Or leave it out.** |
| Lalitaditya beat the Arabs in Punjab | Disputed (Kalhana exaggerates). | "Kashmir ki parampara ye bhi kehti hai..." or leave it out. |
| Arab soldiers fled because of Indian revolt vs internal mutiny | Blankinship thinks internal causes are more likely. | Give both. |

## 5. Quotes we can use (primary sources, via translations)

- **Al-Baladhuri, Futuh al-Buldan (9th c.), Elliot & Dowson tr.:** "Hakim, son of 'Awana al Kalbi, succeeded Tamim. The
  people of India had returned to idolatry excepting those of Kassa, and the Musulmans had no place of security in which
  they could take refuge, so he built a town on the other side of the lake facing India, and called it Al Mahfuza, 'The
  secure'." [S18] → Hinglish narration: *"Arab itihaskar Baladhuri khud likhta hai: Musalmanon ke paas sar chhupane ki
  koi surakshit jagah nahi bachi thi."*
- **Navsari plate (739):** the Tajikas came after defeating the Saindhavas, Kachchhelas, Saurashtra, Chavotakas,
  Mauryas and Gurjaras, and Avanijanashraya defeated them. [S13] (paraphrase, not a verbatim line)
- **Gwalior inscription (Mihira Bhoja, 9th c.) on Nagabhata I:** "crushed the large army of the powerful Mlechcha
  king." [S8]
- **Sulaiman (851):** "no other Indian prince has so fine a cavalry"; he "is unfriendly to the Arabs." [S8][S15]
- **Hajjaj's order (via Wikipedia):** kill the combatants, take their families as hostages, and grant peace and
  protection to those who did not fight. [S6] (balance: the conquest was harsh but had rules)

## 8. Balance and sensitivity notes

- Frame it as **an empire against regional kingdoms**, not "Hindu vs Muslim". Arab merchants lived peacefully in
  Rashtrakuta ports, and Arab writers praised Indian kings.
- Show both sides: the conquest of Sindh was harsh (Debal), but Hajjaj's orders also protected people who did not fight.
  Umar II offered kings their thrones if they converted.
- Make the Arab-vs-Turk difference clear in the verdict, so viewers do not take away "no Muslim ruler ever came".
- Label flags and symbols "PRATIK (andaazan)", and label portraits "Aadhunik chitran".
